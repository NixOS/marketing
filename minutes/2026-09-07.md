## Brand Identity Rework

### Old

We believe in open-source innovation and a **community-driven** ethos — values that have shaped our identity from the very beginning.
As you explore this guide, we hope you’ll sense the spirit of **stable evolution** — a core principle we embrace over stagnation or chaos.

This guide serves as a framework to help ensure that our communication and design consistently reflect the values that define Nix: **innovation**, **reliability**, and **simplicity**.
It brings together **creative expression** and **technical precision** to foster a **unified identity** across all touchpoints.

We’ve aimed to include everything you need to feel confident and comfortable when working with the NixOS visual identity — the public face of our declarative builds and deployments ecosystem.If anything is unclear or you have questions, we’re always happy to help.

Please feel free to reach out to the Marketing Team or the Brand and Design Steward directly. You can find contact information here: nixos.org/community/teams/marketing

    - With a blank slate you can make decisions you otherwise couldn't
    - New people have to get familiar with the culture the incremental develompent embodies
  - @fricklerhandwerk: Problems with clean slate:
    - Conways law: You can't make groups use the thing you want them to use, unless you serve their needs (which is something very different)
      - But that also means you have to maintain it forever
    - Redoing something means pulling through from clean slate to "replaces the existing thing well enough", which is almost always much more time than anyone has available
    - Clean slate approaches risk breaking _some_ workflows almost 100%
      - And this is not about heating spacebars, but this: the real value is created by maintainers, not beginners. Breaking their workflows loses cohesion and support, and that destroys value in the commons
